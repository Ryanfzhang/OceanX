# Comparing research directions

Use for an open-ended research question with competing explanations, not a routine plot.

## Evidence gap
Separate what is observed from what remains unexplained. Useful gaps include conflicting
measurements, unresolved scales, an untested assumption, or an alternative explanation.
An absent dataset is an evidence limit, not a refutation.

For a paper-grounded idea, attribute the findings or limitations actually read and distinguish
them from your own synthesis. A missing topic in the reviewed papers does not prove novelty;
novelty remains provisional until a targeted literature comparison supports it. A search snippet
does not establish the methods or conclusions of unread full text.

## Scientific value
A useful question addresses something the current evidence leaves unresolved, and its answer
would change the explanation or the next research choice. A negative or inconclusive answer
can be informative. If several plausible explanations remain compatible with the observations,
that uncertainty is part of the result, not a reason to claim all explanations were tested.
Keep exploratory findings distinct from predictions specified before examining the data.
