# Evidence-grounded scientific writing

Use when writing a result, caption or scientific interpretation.

Connect each consequential statement to a paper, saved diagnostic or figure with an exact
reference. Distinguish measured quantities, interpretations under assumptions and untested ideas.

## Attribution and uncertainty
"Consistent with" is not "demonstrates." A budget residual is not an independently measured
forcing; missing diagnostics cannot rule out a mechanism. Name alternative explanations that
remain compatible with the evidence. Numerical agreement validates a calculation, not causation.

When estimates disagree, state their definitions and evidence rather than selecting a preferred
story. After a correction, update dependent claims and identify the superseded result.
If the disagreement remains, include it beside the affected conclusion.

## Scope
Match a claim's time window, region and depth to its evidence. A period mean does not establish
the same sign throughout an event; a regional mean does not exclude local contributions.
For sign-changing terms, distinguish the phases or regions relevant to the question.
State the aggregation and baseline instead of silently selecting a favorable window.

Use only supported precision and cite current evidence. A negative result is a legitimate answer.
Missing evidence and execution interruption are limitations, not scientific refutations.
